What to record in a trading journal

A journal is only useful if you can learn from it. These are the ten things worth writing down about every trade, kept short enough that you will still do it on a bad day.

The ten fields

  1. Instrument and direction. What you traded and whether you were long or short.
  2. The plan, before you click. Your entry, your stop (where you are wrong) and your target.
  3. Size and money at risk. As a percentage of the account, not just a number of lots.
  4. The exit and the real result. After fees, swaps and commissions, as your broker shows it.
  5. The result in R. Profit or loss divided by the money you risked. It lets you compare trades of different sizes.
  6. The setup. The name of the pattern or rule you traded, so you can count how each one performs.
  7. The reason, in one sentence. Why this trade, why now.
  8. How you felt. Calm, bored, anxious, chasing. Before the trade and after it.
  9. Mistakes. Did you move the stop, add to a loser, trade outside your hours, skip your plan?
  10. A picture of the chart. At the entry and at the exit. In a month you will not remember what you saw.

Review once a week

Pick the same time each week and look at four things: the win rate and the average R for each setup, your biggest mistake by cost, the weekday and hour at which you lose money, and whether you kept to your daily loss limit. Change one thing at a time, and look again after twenty trades, not after two.
